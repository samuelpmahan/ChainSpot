# NuThing P2 synthetic digit augmentation

Generated by `scripts/nuthing/synthesize-digits.py` (renders + varies digit masks,
classical image processing only -- no generative-image models) and
`scripts/nuthing/normalize-synthetic.ts` (pipes every mask through the
canonical `src/lib/nuthing/digits/normalize.ts`, same normalizer real digit
samples go through in `scripts/nuthing/build-digit-dataset.ts`).

## Why synthetic data

The real grounded digit corpus (`resources/nuthing-p2/digits/digit-dataset.json`)
has only 194 `train`-split samples across 10 classes, heavily skewed toward
`1` (82 samples) with as few as 7 samples for `0` and `9`. Synthetic samples
exist to give a future classifier a larger, class-balanced training signal
calibrated to the real badges' measured geometry -- not to replace real data
for evaluation. See **Provenance separation** below.

## Measured real-glyph stats

Measured with `cv2.connectedComponentsWithStats` + `cv2.distanceTransform`
over every glyph-mask connected component (area >= 3px, noise dropped) across
**all 188 badges / 283 digit components** in
`resources/nuthing-p2/badges/manifest.json` (a first pass sampled 22 badges
across 11 of the 14 source images per the task brief; the full-corpus pass
below is a superset of that and is what calibration actually uses).

| stat | n | min | p10 | median | mean | p90 | max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| component height (px) | 283 | 21.00 | 21.00 | 21.00 | 21.00 | 21.00 | 21.00 |
| component width (px) | 283 | 7.00 | 7.00 | 14.00 | 11.50 | 16.00 | 16.00 |
| stroke thickness (px, `distanceTransform.max() * 2`) | 283 | 4.39 | 4.39 | 5.60 | 5.09 | 5.60 | 5.60 |

Notable finding: **glyph height is not a distribution in this corpus, it is a
constant** -- every one of the 283 digit components across all 14 source
images measures exactly 21px tall. This falls out of the badge crops
themselves: every source image's badge bbox is ~54-57 x 42px (checked
per-image min/max in the manifest), so all badges in this corpus were
captured/rendered at the same physical scale. Width varies by digit shape
(`1` narrow at 7px, `0`/`8`-style round digits up to 16px). Stroke thickness
is bimodal (4.39px / 5.60px) rather than continuous -- an artifact of the
`distanceTransform` kernel's discrete output at this pixel scale, not evidence
of two different font weights in the corpus.

Because the real distribution collapses to a single height value, the
generator does not literally replay `min..max`; instead it samples a small
window centered on the measured 21px (`HEIGHT_PX_RANGE = (18, 24)` in
`synthesize-digits.py`) so the synthetic set stays anchored to what was
actually measured while still covering plausible scale jitter a classifier
might see from crop/scale-pipeline drift in future captures at other
resolutions. This is a deliberate deviation from the task brief's illustrative
`18-30px` range, made *because* measurement showed the real range is much
tighter than assumed -- the brief's number was a placeholder pending
measurement, and measurement is what calibration should follow.

## Fonts used

`fc-list` on this environment was checked for locally available **bold** sans
faces. Three were found and used (roughly evenly split across the 2500
samples, per `index.json`'s font tally: `FreeSansBold.ttf` 846,
`LiberationSans-Bold.ttf` 832, `DejaVuSans-Bold.ttf` 822):

- `DejaVuSans-Bold.ttf` (`/usr/share/fonts/truetype/dejavu/DejaVuSans-Bold.ttf`)
- `LiberationSans-Bold.ttf` (`/usr/share/fonts/truetype/liberation/LiberationSans-Bold.ttf`)
- `FreeSansBold.ttf` (`/usr/share/fonts/truetype/freefont/FreeSansBold.ttf`)

All three are bold, non-condensed grotesque sans faces, a reasonable match
for UDisc's white bold sans-serif badge digits without needing a
metrics-matched proprietary font.

## Generation pipeline (`scripts/nuthing/synthesize-digits.py`)

Each font+digit pair is rendered once at a large reference point size
(200pt) and tight-cropped to its ink bounding box (`render_ref_glyph`). Every
sample then applies, in order, on top of that cached reference glyph:

1. **Supersampled resize** (`SS = 4x`) to the sample's target height, so the
   later downsample step produces realistic antialiasing rather than a hard
   nearest-neighbor edge.
2. **Rotation**, `rotationDeg ~ Uniform(-2, 2)` -- kept small because the real
   badges are screen-aligned (UDisc renders them upright); this only models
   sub-degree capture/render jitter, not arbitrary orientation.
3. **Subpixel translation**, `dx, dy ~ Uniform(-0.5, 0.5)` low-res pixel
   units, applied as an integer offset at the 4x-supersampled scale so it
   becomes a true fractional shift once downsampled -- this is what lets
   binarization threshold variation actually sample different points along
   the antialiased edge instead of always landing on the same rounded pixel.
4. **Downsample** to final physical scale (Lanczos).
5. **Gaussian blur**, `sigma ~ Uniform(0, 0.8)` -- models slight optical/JPEG
   softening; sigma near 0 is a no-op, so most samples get a small amount and
   a minority get a visible amount.
6. **JPEG round-trip**, applied to ~35% of samples (`JPEG_PROB = 0.35`),
   `quality ~ randint(40, 90)` -- models the compression artifacts real
   captures may have gone through.
7. **Binarize** at `threshold ~ Uniform(90, 165)` (of 255) -- centered near
   the antialiasing midpoint (128) so the sampled threshold actually walks
   across the AA boundary the way real threshold/exposure variation would.
8. **Morphology**: 60% none, 20% erode, 20% dilate (3x3 kernel, 1 iteration)
   -- models stroke-weight variation from rendering/compression, with erosion
   guarded to never erase a glyph down to nothing.
9. **Partial obstruction**, applied to ~15% of samples (measured actual rate:
   16.2%, `OBSTRUCTION_PROB = 0.15` before the coverage-cap backoff can skip
   a would-be obstruction that can't fit under the cap on a very small
   glyph): overlays a line or filled rectangle of background color, capped at
   removing **at most 20%** of the glyph's currently-set pixels via a fixed
   shrink schedule (no extra randomness spent backing off, to keep the RNG
   draw sequence identical across runs) -- models real badges getting crossed
   by a rendered course-path line.
10. **Tight bounding-box crop** of the final binary mask, saved as an 8-bit
    grayscale PNG (0/255, matching the real `glyphMask` convention: 255 =
    bright digit pixel).

Observed rates across the generated 2500 samples: JPEG round-trip 34.6%,
obstruction 16.2%, morphology none/erode/dilate 59.5%/20.0%/20.5%.

## Determinism

All randomness comes from a single `numpy.random.RandomState(SEED)` (`SEED =
20260819`, a fixed constant), consumed in a fixed order (digit `0..9`, sample
index `0..249`, and within each sample a fixed sequence of parameter draws
regardless of which branches those parameters end up taking). No wall-clock
time, OS entropy, filesystem iteration order, or hash-based container
iteration feeds into sample content or ordering.

Verified: ran `python3 scripts/nuthing/synthesize-digits.py` twice back to
back and compared `sha256sum` over `index.json` and every generated PNG --
**byte-identical** on both runs (2500/2500 PNGs + `index.json` matched).

## Output schema

`scripts/nuthing/synthesize-digits.py` writes
`/workspace/nuthing-work/digits/synthetic-raw/` (2500 PNGs + `index.json`,
untracked scratch space -- regenerate by rerunning the script).

`scripts/nuthing/normalize-synthetic.ts` reads that raw output, normalizes
every mask through `normalizeDigitMask` (`DIGIT_W=24, DIGIT_H=32`,
aspect-preserving nearest-neighbor, centered), and writes:

- `/workspace/nuthing-work/digits/synthetic-dataset.json` -- all 2500
  normalized samples (untracked scratch space; regenerate by rerunning both
  scripts in order).
- `resources/nuthing-p2/digits/synthetic-dataset.sample.json` -- **committed**
  first-5-per-digit subset (50 samples) so reviewers can inspect the schema
  without pulling the full generated file.

Sample schema (both files):

```json
{
  "generator": "scripts/nuthing/synthesize-digits.py + normalize-synthetic.ts",
  "samples": [
    {
      "id": "synth#1",
      "split": "synthetic",
      "provenanceClass": "SYNTHETIC",
      "trueDigit": "0",
      "mask": "<base64, digitToBase64() of a 24x32 normalized mask>",
      "params": {
        "font": "FreeSansBold.ttf",
        "heightPx": 20.7332,
        "translation": { "dx": -0.0625, "dy": 0.4022 },
        "threshold": 141.6,
        "morph": "dilate",
        "blurSigma": 0.6931,
        "jpegQuality": 74,
        "rotationDeg": -0.4855,
        "obstruction": null
      }
    }
  ]
}
```

## Provenance separation (synthetic vs. real)

Synthetic samples are marked `"split": "synthetic"` and
`"provenanceClass": "SYNTHETIC"` -- distinct from the real dataset's
`split: "train" | "heldout"` and `provenanceClass: "REAL_GROUNDED" |
"UNRESOLVED" | "HELD_OUT"` (`scripts/nuthing/build-digit-dataset.ts`,
`docs/nuthing-p2/digit-segmentation.md`). Any classifier or evaluation script
consuming both files must filter on these fields explicitly; **synthetic
samples must never be scored as evaluation truth**, and real `HELD_OUT`
samples must never be used for training synthetic or otherwise. The two
datasets are written to separate files specifically so a training script has
to opt in to combining them rather than accidentally concatenating
directories.
